# Tips and Tricks: Getting Started with AI

You do not need to code. You do not need to install anything. You need curiosity and a healthy habit of checking the answer.

## What these things are

- **A large language model (LLM)** is the AI you chat with. Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini are examples. It writes by predicting what words should come next, based on a huge amount of reading.
- **An agent** is an LLM that can also *do* things: read your files, make documents, search the web, save to Google Drive. You tell it a goal and it works through the steps.

Think of it as a very fast, very eager assistant who has read nearly everything and sometimes confidently gets things wrong.

## Your first five minutes

1. Open any AI assistant in your browser.
2. Attach a document and ask: *"Summarize this in five bullet points, then tell me what you were unsure about."*
3. Read the answer against the original.

That last step is the whole game.

## Five habits that make everything better

**1. Be specific.** "Make this better" gets a guess. "Make this easier for a third grader to read, keep every word, and put it in a Google Doc" gets a result.

**2. Give it the real material.** Attach the actual file. Do not describe it from memory.

**3. Say what must not change.** Tell it what is locked. "Do not change any wording" is a perfectly good sentence.

**4. Ask it to be honest about itself.** Try: *"Which parts of your answer are certain, which are guesses, and how could I check each one?"*

**5. Keep going.** The first answer is a draft. Say what is wrong and ask again. "Make the headings larger." "Put it back the way it was." Chat answers are easy to redo. Files are different: if an agent has access to your Drive, have it work on a copy, and ask it to check with you before it changes, overwrites, shares, or deletes anything of yours.

## Three ways to work with AI

You can start at the first and move up whenever you feel ready.

1. **Ask.** "Explain this to me." "Give me five ideas." You read the answer.
2. **Create.** "Use my document to make a better-looking version." You get a draft and review it.
3. **Delegate.** "Make the document, check it against the original, save it to my Drive, and tell me what to double-check." The agent does the steps. You still do the final check.

The document makeover in this repository is level 3, and it works because the instructions say exactly what must not change.

## Prompts worth copying

> **Check yourself.** Which parts of your last answer are things you are certain of, which are guesses, and what could I do to check each one?

> **Say it simpler.** Explain that again for someone who has never used this. One short paragraph, no jargon.

> **What could go wrong?** Before I do this, what could it change or delete, and how would I undo it?

> **Compare.** Compare your version to the original line by line. List every difference.

> **Ask me first.** Before you start, ask me any questions you need answered. Then begin.

## Where AI goes wrong

- **It sounds sure when it is not.** Confident tone is not evidence.
- **It invents things.** Names, sources, statistics, quotes. If it matters, look it up somewhere else.
- **It quietly "fixes" things.** Ask it to copy a document and it may improve a word you wanted left alone. This is why the template says *locked content* and asks for a line-by-line check.
- **It says it saved a file when it did not.** Open the file yourself.
- **It forgets.** Long conversations drift. If it starts getting sloppy, begin a new chat and paste in what matters.

## Privacy

- Do not paste or upload student names, photos, grades, handwriting, or anything protected.
- Check your institution's policy before using AI with anything official.
- Treat anything you type into an AI as something that might be stored.
- Look at what you are about to upload before you upload it.

## Which tool should I use?

Try two or three on the same task and compare. They have different strengths, and they change quickly. Ask each one the same question and see whose answer you trust. Ask the AI itself what it can do. It will tell you, and you should still check.

## Agents and Google Drive

Some agents can connect to your Google Drive and save finished files there. Others cannot, and will hand you a file instead. Either is fine. If you get a file, upload it to Drive and open it with Google Docs.

If an agent says it saved something to Drive, **open Drive and look.**

## A good rule

> **Ask the computer to explain itself before you follow its instructions.**

If you remember nothing else, remember that. Then enjoy it. These tools are genuinely useful, occasionally funny, and a lot less scary once you have watched one get something wrong and caught it.
