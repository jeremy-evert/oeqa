# MAKE THIS INTO A PHONE-READY LEARNING GAME (Template v2)

Community Anchors. A copy-ready playbook for turning one classroom worksheet into something a child wants to open on a phone, and a teacher can trust.

Version 2 was written after an independent test of version 1 (Roll and Read, 3rd grade). That test scored "teacher independence" 2 out of 5, found three vague rules, and caught damaged file uploads. Every change below answers one of those findings.

---

## START HERE: THE 2-MINUTE VERSION (no coding, no downloads)

1. Copy this doc.
2. Type your worksheet text into the brief (Part A). Do not upload photos with names or handwriting.
3. Tell your AI assistant what it can really make (Part B, Stage 0).
4. Paste the Master Prompt (Part C) and your brief.
5. Open what it makes **on a phone**. Run the checklist (Part F).
6. Hand it to one child. Watch. Do not coach.

You choose the route by what your AI can actually produce:

| Your AI can make | Route | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| A web page (HTML) | **Phone Quest** | Tap-to-play game. Best for excitement. |
| A PDF file | **Phone Deck** | Swipeable 9:16 pages, read-and-say. No tapping games. |
| Only text | **Docs Only** | A Google Doc with big type. File > Download > PDF. |
| A PDF for paper | **Print Packet** | US Letter, grayscale friendly (v1 route). |

Everything below works with any of them.

## PART B: THE FIDELITY CONTRACT (what "faithful" means)

**Locked.** These must match the original exactly, character for character: every word in every cell, every number, every row and column label, every direction, every rule of the game, the title.

**Allowed.** Type size, spacing, colors, borders, icons, sound, animation, buttons, rewards, writing room, and a read-aloud button.

**Must be listed.** Anything you add to the original activity itself (a check box in each cell, a letter-tile spelling step, a highlight on letter pairs) goes in a "Changes from the original" list and is tagged NEW on screen.

**Never.** Silent word swaps. Invented answer keys. Claims that a method improves reading. Standards alignment unless a teacher confirmed it. Student names, photos or handwriting.

## PART C: THE MASTER PROMPT (copy everything in this box)

> You are my educational editor, instructional designer and production assistant. Turn my worksheet into a phone-ready game a third grader will want to play, while keeping the teacher's activity exactly as written. Work in stages. Never silently change a word, number, direction or rule.
>
> **STAGE 0. CAPABILITIES.** Before anything else, tell me in one sentence each: can you create (a) an HTML page I can open on a phone, (b) a PDF file, (c) a Google Doc, (d) only text? Say which you can truly do and which you cannot. Do not pretend. Pick the best route from the table in my template.
>
> **STAGE 1. UNDERSTAND.** Transcribe the original into a review table. Mark anything unsure as [VERIFY]. List the locked items. In plain words, say what the child does, step by step. Do not use student names or handwriting.
>
> **STAGE 2. DESIGN.** Offer two directions: (A) calm and clear, (B) adventurous. Same words, same tasks. For a phone, follow the engagement rules in Part D. Say what each choice costs in readability.
>
> **STAGE 3. BUILD.** Build the locked activity first, as written. Then add at most three optional extras, each tagged NEW and "not teacher-reviewed". Give every extra a plain name.
>
> **STAGE 4. VERIFY.** Run a mechanical check: every locked word and direction appears exactly once as written (a script, or a line-by-line table). Check on a phone-width screen. Check privacy. List every change from the original. List what needs a teacher's review (word sorting, phonics claims, answer keys).
>
> **STAGE 5. SAVE AND PROVE IT.** Save each file. Then open or read each one back and confirm every page is there. A file that has the right size can still be damaged. Tell me exactly what you saved, where, and what you could not save. Never say a file exists unless you read it back.
>
> **STAGE 6. DELIVER.** Give me: the phone version, a printable version if I asked, the editable source, the changes list, a one-paragraph note for a parent, and three questions to ask the child.
>
> My brief: [PASTE PART A]. The original: [PASTE TYPED TEXT].

## PART D: ENGAGEMENT RULES FOR A 3RD GRADER ON A PHONE

1. **One job per screen.** One big button. Never two ways to get lost.
2. **Touch targets at least 48 pixels.** Small hands, moving thumbs.
3. **Hear it.** A read-aloud button on every word. Hide it if the phone cannot speak.
4. **Roll, don't click.** A tumbling die beats a "Next" button. Also let the child tap the number from a real die.
5. **Fast, kind feedback.** Sparkle and a chime for effort. A wrong answer shows "which letter is different?" and never a buzzer, a lost life or a timer.
6. **Make it a trail.** Show where they are (Row A to Row D) and what is left.
7. **Small rewards.** Stars and tally marks that match the teacher's sheet. No leaderboards, no names.
8. **Spelling by tapping.** Letter tiles beat typing for beginners. Keep a "spell it out loud or on paper" option, because that is what the sheet says.
9. **Dyslexia-friendly type.** Large, open letters, plenty of space. Never rely on color alone: pair every color with a label, shape or underline.
10. **Say what is new.** Tag every addition NEW. A child does not care. A teacher does.
11. **Privacy by default.** Nothing typed or drawn leaves the phone. Store only a star count, if anything.

## PART F: THE FINAL QUALITY GATE

Content
- [ ] Every locked word, number and direction matches the original (script or line-by-line table).
- [ ] Every addition is tagged NEW and listed under "Changes from the original".
- [ ] A named teacher has reviewed word sorting and phonics claims, or each page says "Not reviewed".
- [ ] No answer key is invented.

Phone
- [ ] Opened on a real phone in portrait. Nothing scrolls sideways. Text is readable without zooming.
- [ ] Every button is big enough for a thumb.
- [ ] The game works with sound off and with read-aloud missing.
- [ ] Color is never the only signal.

Files
- [ ] Each saved file was read back and every page or screen is present.
- [ ] Fonts are embedded, or standard fonts only, and a test print or screen check looks right.
- [ ] Editable source and word table are saved next to the output.

People
- [ ] No student names, photos or handwriting anywhere.
- [ ] Permission and attribution written down. Sharing set on purpose.
- [ ] One child tried it without coaching. One caregiver understood the directions. One teacher confirmed the objective.

FINISH LINE: A child asks to play again. A parent understands it in ten seconds. A teacher trusts it. Another educator can make the next one without you.
