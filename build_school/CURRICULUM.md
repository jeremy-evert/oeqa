# Curriculum: 21 small steps in five chapters

**This is a roadmap, not a claim.** Only lessons marked *written* exist. The others are goals to be built, tested and corrected one at a time.

Stop anywhere. After Step 04 you can question an AI answer. After Step 12 you can make and recover small software changes.

| Step | Chapter | Title | Goal | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 00 | Doorway | Ask one better question | Ask AI to explain a harmless command without running it | written, untested |
| 01 | Discover | Meet your computer | CPU, memory, storage and operating system, in plain words | roadmap |
| 02 | Discover | Before you press Enter | Ask what a command reads, changes or risks | roadmap |
| 03 | Discover | Challenge the answer | Find one claim that needs independent evidence | draft, untested |
| 04 | Discover | Read the evidence | Compare what you expected with what actually printed | roadmap |
| 05 to 08 | Get comfortable building | (to be named) | Turn curiosity into small, manageable actions | roadmap |
| 09 to 12 | Learn engineering habits | (to be named) | Protect progress, record changes, establish proof | roadmap |
| 13 to 16 | Build with AI | (to be named) | Give AI clear work while keeping control | roadmap |
| 17 to 20 | Become an independent builder | (to be named) | Grow into real projects and optional orchestration | roadmap |

## The pattern behind Steps 13 to 16 (from the plan)

**Goal → Baseline → AI-assisted change → Inspect the differences → Prove → Commit.**

This is more useful than "ask AI to write code", because the learner keeps control at every step. Source: the author's `aider_day_00` exercise (to be re-verified before use).

## Chapter ideas to harvest (not yet verified)

Plan, challenge, build, independently verify, record. Durable prompts, reports and checkpoints. Confidence ratings and reflection. Approval gates and observe-before-change. See `sidecar/source_harvest.md`.

## Not in the beginner path

Multi-agent frameworks, Docker, local LLM setups, large install scripts, private project histories. The first release works in a browser with ordinary tools and no paid subscription.
